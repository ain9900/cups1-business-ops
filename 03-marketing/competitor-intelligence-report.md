# 1. Executive summary

Nine competitors were studied across four business models. Together they show how this market is won: **low minimums or low prices pull buyers in, free samples and quote forms capture them, and email, ads and retargeting keep them.**

- **Four models compete for the same café buyer.** They are (A) online custom-print brands (YourBrandCafe, LogoCup, CupPrint, noissue), (B) eco-packaging distributors (Good Start Packaging, KimEcopak), (C) large national distributors (Karat Packaging) and (D) Asian factories selling direct (YOON, and Sowinpak itself).
- **The best financial evidence is Karat Packaging (Nasdaq: KRT).** It had Q1 2026 net sales of $116.9M (+12.9%), and online sales were about 17% of that. Tariffs raised its import costs from 8.6% to 13.8% of sales. The market is growing, but tariffs squeeze margins.
- **The market splits on speed versus price.** Digital printers ship 50–1,000 cups in 10–15 days at $0.38–0.50+ per cup. Factory and overseas programs need 10,000–50,000 cups and 8–16 weeks, at about $0.06–0.15 per cup.
- **Every serious competitor offers free samples** (Good Start, LogoCup, YOON, KimEcopak, noissue). A sample program is the standard way to capture leads.
- **Most US/Canadian competitors run heavy tracking stacks.** They use Google Tag Manager, GA4, Google Ads, the Meta and TikTok pixels, Microsoft Clarity session recording, HubSpot or Klaviyo, and SMS marketing. Visitors are retargeted and put into email flows.
- **Our own supplier sells direct.** Sowinpak's website targets brands worldwide with quote forms, live chat and WhatsApp. Cup1 must add value the factory can't: a delivered US/Canada price, compliance documents, a local point of contact, and inspection.
- **Nobody leads with "compliance documented and delivered at factory price".** The Phase 3 positioning still holds.

# 3. How they work – four business models

**A. Online custom-print brands.** These sell design-and-print as a service. The customer uploads a logo or designs online, approves a proof, pays, and gets cups in 10–21 days. Margins come from convenience and small runs. Printing is often digital (LogoCup), which makes very low minimums possible. noissue owns no factories: it runs a platform on top of manufacturing partners in the US, Canada, UK, Europe, China, Vietnam and Australia. That is the same asset-light model as Cup1.

**B. Eco distributors.** These sell many brands of compostable packaging from stock, plus a custom-print program made overseas or by partners. Good Start "does not manufacture products and focuses on sales and marketing" (Business NH, 2020). KimEcopak adds services such as a 6-month fixed price, free storage and scheduled delivery.

**C. National distributor/manufacturer.** Karat runs 12 US warehouses (Texas, California, New Jersey, Illinois, Washington, Hawaii, South Carolina, Arizona, Florida). It sells to chains and distributors ($92.9M in Q1 2026), online ($19.5M) and through retail ($4.5M). Custom print is an add-on with a 12–14 week lead time on overseas production.

**D. Factory-direct exporters.** These are Asian factories that sell straight to Western brands through SEO content, Alibaba and trade shows. They have the lowest price and the highest minimums, and the buyer handles import, duties and risk.

**Where Cup1 fits:** an asset-light model like noissue and Good Start, with factory-direct pricing close to model D, plus the landed price and compliance service that model D does not offer.

**How the data is used:**

1. **Capture.** Quote forms, sample requests, account sign-ups, newsletter and discount pop-ups (Privy), and logo uploads collect the email, company, location and order size.
2. **Identify.** HubSpot and Clearbit match a visitor's company even before a form is filled in. LinkedIn Insight shows job titles and companies.
3. **Watch.** Microsoft Clarity and Hotjar record sessions and heatmaps to fix drop-off on quote and checkout pages.
4. **Retarget.** Meta, TikTok, Google, Microsoft, LinkedIn and Pinterest pixels show ads to past visitors.
5. **Nurture.** Klaviyo, HubSpot and Omnisend send automated email (abandoned quote or cart, reorder reminders) and SMS.

# 8. Competitor profiles

**YourBrandCafe (Georgia, USA)** – Founded by brothers Bryan and Craig Warner in 2008–09. Low minimums (one case), free proofs, free shipping, and a franchise program (YBC Plus). It also runs YBC Supply for blank cups and gives $1 per case to charity: water. It blogs and posts on five social networks. Threat: owns the small-café, low-minimum segment.

**LogoCup (USA)** – Launched in 2025 by BoxUp. Full-color digital printing from 50 cups with a 10-day turnaround, an online designer, and "one free cup" to start. It markets through sponsored content in Fresh Cup. Threat: events and very small cafés; not a 30k+ competitor.

**CupPrint USA (South Bend, Indiana; Huhtamaki group)** – A manufacturer with US and Irish production. 1,000-cup minimum, 15-day turnaround, FSC-certified cups, free design templates and an in-house design service. Threat: fast US-made custom cups with big-group backing.

**noissue (global, mainly US)** – An asset-light packaging platform with a $10M Series A and 80,000+ customers (2021). It organizes products by industry, sells cups, coffee bags and takeout packaging, and runs a mature growth stack (Clearbit, HubSpot, multi-channel pixels). Threat: a brand-led, sustainable "one-stop" offer.

**Good Start Packaging (Bedford, New Hampshire)** – A Certified B Corp (score 90.7) that distributes compostable brands plus its own line. Custom cups have a 50-case minimum and 14–16 week lead time. It offers free samples, reorder, and a 20% profit donation. Threat: eco credibility. Opportunity: its long lead time.

**KimEcopak (Quebec and Alberta, Canada)** – Founder-led eco distributor with custom cups from 10,000, free 3D mockups, a 6-month fixed price, free storage and scheduled delivery. It is expanding into US warehouses and publishes heavy SEO content. Threat: the closest model to Cup1 in Canada.

**Karat Packaging (Chino, California; Nasdaq: KRT)** – National distributor and manufacturer with 12 US warehouses. Q1 2026 sales were $116.9M, with online about 17%. Custom print has a 12–14 week lead time and serves chains. Threat: the price and scale benchmark for chains.

**YOON (Wuhan, China)** – Factory exporter targeting US buyers with comparison articles. Claims $0.06–0.08 per 12oz cup, a 10,000 minimum, free samples, WhatsApp and live chat. Threat: price anchor for buyers who can import themselves.

**Sowinpak (Wenzhou, China; plants in Vietnam and Indonesia)** – Our Phase 2 supplier also sells direct to brands, with quote forms, a catalog, Tidio chat and WhatsApp. It claims 2,000+ brands. Risk: clients could bypass Cup1 and go to the factory, so Cup1's service layer must be worth paying for.

# 9. Lessons and implications for Cup1

**Opportunities**

1. Compete in the **30,000–100,000 cup** range, where digital printers are too expensive and factories are too hard to buy from directly.
2. Lead with **"delivered price + compliance documents"**. No competitor leads with documented FDA/PFAS compliance, and US buyers face state PFAS bans.
3. Beat **Good Start and Karat on lead time** (35 days plus shipping versus 12–16 weeks).
4. Use **Vietnam production** as a tariff advantage, especially for Canada while the China anti-dumping case runs.

**Threats**

1. **Factory disintermediation.** Sowinpak and YOON sell direct. Protect Cup1 with service (a local contact, landed price, QC, documents), client ownership and, if possible, supplier non-circumvention terms.
2. **Low-minimum convenience.** YourBrandCafe and LogoCup win small cafés. Cup1 should not chase orders under 10,000 cups.
3. **Proof gap.** Competitors show reviews, client logos and B Corp badges. Cup1 has none yet, so its first 3–5 clients should become case studies.

**What Cup1 should copy for Phase 4 and Phase 5**

- A free sample program (our Sample Kit) and a "delivered quote" form with a logo upload.
- Clear minimums and lead times on the homepage.
- A tracking setup: Google Tag Manager, GA4, Microsoft Clarity (free), the Meta and LinkedIn pixels, and a CRM (HubSpot free tier) with email automation. Add a cookie-consent banner.
- An SEO blog on compliance (PFAS, compostable labeling, tariffs) and city-level "custom cups" pages for the US and Canada.
- Reorder reminders and a referral program once clients exist.

**Effect on Phase 3 branding:** the findings support the approved positioning (eco + compliant, factory-direct, delivered). Two additions are recommended: show the minimum (from 30,000) and the lead time (about 35 days plus shipping) prominently, and plan a "Request a Sample Kit" call to action alongside "Get a delivered quote". No change to the name, tagline or visual identity is needed.

# 10. Method and limits

- Desk research on 5 November 2026: company websites, press releases, trade press and SEC-filed press releases.
- Website technology was detected by inspecting each site's live page code in a browser. YourBrandCafe and YOON block automated browsers, so they were profiled from their page content only. Tags behind Google Tag Manager may be under-counted.
- Private competitors do not publish revenue. Their figures are self-reported or come from older press articles, and are labeled with dates.
- The Q2 2026 Karat figures come from an AI-generated earnings summary (Quartr) and should be checked against the company's 10-Q.
